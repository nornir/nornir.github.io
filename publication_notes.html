Publication notes: nornir

Image processing, registration and volume build pipeline for serial-section electron microscopy (the nornir packages), including the export of training data for the SAM2 segmentation trainer. Material for future papers: citations, formulas, algorithmic decisions and the reasoning behind them. Index of all projects: ~/.cursor/publication-notes.md. Format and rules: the publication-notes skill.

Methods and contributions

  • Section registration and volume assembly (nornir-imageregistration, nornir-buildmanager): to fill with the registration, stos and mosaicking methods as they are written up.

  • Segmentation training export (nornir-buildmanager/nornir_buildmanager/operations/segmentationtraining): crops, masks, circle_annotations.json and shapes.jsonl per volume for the trainer. Circles are written at their true radii, either as annotated or as the polygon’s inscribed circle (the largest circle inside the exterior ring, holes ignored); scaling a circle into a prompt is the trainer’s job, never the exporter’s.

  • Carried prompts at export (planned) (Sam2Trainer docs/adjacent_circle_seed_plan.md): circles and polygons linked to a target polygon within two sections are carried into the target’s section at export (section transform, published residual correction, inverse transform) and stored on the target’s shapes.jsonl line as linked_circles and linked_polygons, with their overlap with the target and a provenance record (source position, size, LastModified, and a thumbprint of the section-to-section mapping) so only changed carries are recomputed.

  • Noise-crushed import (nornir-imageregistration/nornir_imageregistration/noise_crush.py, ImportIDoc/ImportMRC): raw 16-bit TEM tiles are stored as section-wide noise-step codes, $q = \lfloor (x - o)/\Delta \rfloor$ with $\Delta = k\hat\sigma$, instead of contrast-stretched 8-bit. About 86% smaller than raw uint16 at $k = 2.5$; the step and estimator are recorded in each PNG and on the filter so counts can be recovered to within $\Delta/2$.

Formulas

Noise sigma per tile, Donoho-Johnstone MAD of the orthonormal Haar diagonal subband over an even-aligned center crop of at most 2048²: $h = (a - b - c + d)/2$ for each 2x2 block, $\hat\sigma = \mathrm{median}(|h - \mathrm{median}(h)|)/0.6745$. Section $\hat\sigma$ is the median over up to 16 evenly sampled tiles. Crush: $\Delta = \max(1, \mathrm{round}(k\hat\sigma))$, $q = \min(\lfloor \max(x - o, 0)/\Delta \rfloor, 2^b - 1)$, $o = \min(0, \lfloor x_{\min} \rfloor)$ for signed data else 0; recovery $\hat x = q\Delta + \Delta/2 + o$. Added error: RMS $\Delta/\sqrt{12}$ ($0.72\hat\sigma$ at $k = 2.5$).

Circle carry, five-point mean scale (Sam2Trainer docs/depth_relative_trust.md, section 7): $c’ = \Phi(c)$, $R’ = R \cdot \frac{1}{4} \sum_{k=1}^{4} \lVert \Phi(c + R e_k) - \Phi(c) \rVert / R$, with $\Phi = T_t^{-1} \circ C \circ T_s$.

Overlap of a carried shape $X$ with the target $T$: $o = |X \cap T| / |X|$ (whole cell, holes excluded); the trainer keeps $o \ge 0.60$.

Pyre composite view, display to camera solve (nornir-pyre/pyre/views/composite_display.py, lookat_from_display_position): find the source-space camera centre $\ell$ with $T(\ell) = d$ for a display point $d$ by $\ell_0 = T^{-1}(d)$, $\ell_{k+1} = \ell_k + T^{-1}(d) - T^{-1}(T(\ell_k))$, keeping the best $\ell_k$ and stopping when $\lVert T(\ell_k) - d \rVert_\infty \le 0.01$ px or after 8 steps ($T$ maps source to target).

Algorithmic decisions

  • 2026-10-31 Mosaic grid refine measures each vertex with the ir-refine-grid estimator. Why: with the stop rule matched, Python still trailed the 2022 C++ grids by 4-33% seam MAE on RC3 (0800, 1061, 1300, 1637). The port had replaced the legacy match_one_pair with a single-peak phase correlation. The faithful port (refine_shared/legacy_cell_match.py): low-pass both spectra and the Girod-Kuo cross-power spectrum (reproducing the legacy apply_lp_filter bug that adds $s_y$ for $s_y^2$), threshold the PDF to its top $\min(64/A, \max(5/A, 0.01))$ fraction, cluster 8-connected periodic blobs, keep peaks within 2x of the best, reject the vertex when more than 10 remain, then pick among the zero shift and the four wrap permutations of each peak by $-\mathrm{NCC}$ of the moving cell against the whole fixed tile, a pixel counting as valid when inside the tile buffer. RC3 0800 seam MAE 0.0859 / p90 0.1449 vs C++ 0.0857 / 0.1432 (0.1007 / 0.1509 before); Grid690 0.0824 vs golden 0.0901. Rejected: further tuning of the single-peak path. Where: nornir-imageregistration/docs/refine_grid_step_inventory.md rows 15-17.

  • 2026-10-30 Mosaic grid refine stops on the legacy mean shift, in full-resolution pixels. Why: the Python port of ir-refine-grid stopped on the max $|s|$ component (non-monotonic, follows the noisiest vertex) and compared working-resolution shifts with a threshold the C++ tool applies in full-resolution pixels (-sp sets the ITK tile spacing), a 4x looser stop at -sp 4. The stop metric is now $\bar{s} = \frac{d}{2V}\sum_{v}(|s_{v,x}| + |s_{v,y}|)$ over all $V$ vertices of all tiles, $d$ the downsample; refinement stops when $\bar{s} \le \tau$ or $\bar{s}$ fails to fall. RC3 0800: 5 passes, seam MAE 0.1007 (max rule 0.1000 after 4; working-res mean 0.1061 after 2; C++ 0.0857). RC3 1061: 0.0933 against 0.1011 (C++ 0.0831). The stop explains under half the gap to C++; the rest was the per-vertex estimator (2026-10-31 entry above). Rejected: working-res mean (stops after 2 passes). Where: nornir-imageregistration/nornir_imageregistration/local_distortion_correction.py (_refine_tileset), nornir-imageregistration/docs/grid_refine_stos_vs_mosaic.md.

  • 2026-10-30 Grid-refine and translate throughput: process-pool prewarp and pass-wide FFT batches on both backends. Why: on 850-tile RC3 sections the GPU ran at a median 10% SM and the host at 1-2% CPU; CuPy prewarp from threads took 188 s against 17 s for the NumPy process pool (RC3 1061, 2 passes, 128 cores), and per-pair FFT batches held ~121 cells. Output-neutral (bit-identical on Grid690; CuPy vs NumPy 2e-7 px mean on RC3 0800). Rejected: thread-parallel tiles (measured regression, GIL and single CUDA stream). Where: nornir-imageregistration/docs/mosaic_refine_grid_gpu_assessment.md (Full-section throughput).

  • 2026-10-30 Import crushes raw TEM tiles by default (-NoCrush opts out), at $k = 2.5$ true sigma. Why: on three 4080² uint16 tiles (C:\Temp\000-002.png, 2026-09-26 prototype) $k = 2.5$ keeps 34-39 levels (tissue p0.5-p99.5 spans 10-17) at 4.65-4.79 MB, 86% below raw (33.3 MB) and about 56% below today’s stretched 8-bit PNG (~10.5 MB); 10.2 TB of raw tiles would be about 1.45 TB. The September prototype’s “90% at 3 sigma” summed the Haar HH without the 1/2 factor (2 sigma on synthetic $\sigma = 10$) and averaged in a structure-inflated patch estimate, so its step was about 6 true sigma: 15-21 levels and RMS error 1.73 sigma, larger than the noise removed. Rejected: $k = 3$ (86%, 29-34 levels, user preferred the level count of 2.5), $k \approx 6$ (above), per-tile steps (tiles in a section would have different units, breaking the section histogram, contrast and assembly), TIFF+DEFLATE (4.6-5.1 MB vs PNG level 6 4.3-4.4 MB, and a changed tile extension would rewrite every .mosaic and checksum), PNG level 9 (5-6 s/tile). An existing uncrushed Raw8 is never replaced, so re-importing to add sections does not rebuild old ones. Prune scores of crushed tiles are scaled by $255/(q_{\max} - q_{\min})$ of the section cutoffs so existing thresholds keep their meaning. Where: nornir_imageregistration/noise_crush.py, nornir-buildmanager/nornir_buildmanager/importers/{shared,idoc,mrc}.py, operations/pruneobj.py (CrushedScoreScale).

  • 2026-10-26 Pyre holds images deeper than 8 bits as float32; 8-bit images stay float16. Why: float16 keeps 11 significant bits, so a 16-bit image loses about 5 bits in Pyre. Save Warped Image of a real 16-bit TEM tile (nornir-testdata/Images/16-bit/10000.tif, identity transform) matched the source at 34.3% of pixels with errors up to 16 levels when held as float16, and at 100% as float32. float16 represents every 8-bit level exactly, so 8-bit images keep it. Cost: 16-bit images use twice the memory (4080 x 4080: 32 to 64 MiB). Rejected: changing the library default default_image_dtype() (doubles memory in the build pipeline too); re-reading the source file at export time (registration would still see the reduced precision). Where: nornir-pyre/pyre/viewmodels/imageviewmodel.py (working_float_dtype), nornir-pyre/pyre/state/imageloader.py.

  • 2026-10-26 Integer images to float16 are scaled in float32. Why: ImageParamToImageArray cast to float16 and then divided by the integer maximum, but that maximum (65535 for uint16) is above float16’s largest value, 65504. Every 16- or 32-bit image raised an overflow (the package sets np.seterr(over='raise')) or, without that setting, would load as all zeros, so Pyre could not open 16-bit images (the 1.8.1 installer included). Only that case now divides in float32, one row slab at a time; 8-bit images and float32/float64 conversions keep the original expression and are pixel-identical to before on real 8-bit mosaic and Blob images and on 16-bit TEM/SEM tiles; 16-bit to float16 is within 2.4e-4 of a float64 reference. Where: nornir-imageregistration/nornir_imageregistration/core/_core.py (_int_image_to_unit_float, 4358e8d).

  • 2026-10-26 Save Warped Image warps the source image into target space, at the source file’s bit depth. Why: it warped the target image onto a source-sized canvas. On a real StosBrute64 pair (100-101_ctrl-TEM_Blob_map-TEM_Blob.stos) the export now matches TransformStos (correlation 0.998, target shape 683 x 682). The default “PNG 16-bit” choice also wrote 8-bit sources as 16-bit PNGs; the export depth is now capped at the source file’s depth. Where: nornir-pyre/pyre/common.py (warped_image_export), nornir-pyre/pyre/ui/windows/stoswindow.py (onSaveWarpedImage), 4eb7418.

  • 2026-10-26 Save STOS: ControlImageDim from the target image, MappedImageDim from the source. Why: Pyre wrote them swapped (in StosState the deprecated “Fixed” names hold Source), and StosFile.Save keeps preset sizes, so every .stos Pyre saved with differently sized images carried swapped sizes. They feed the grid transform’s bounds and the refine plausibility check. Files saved before nornir-pyre 6cb309f with unequal image sizes keep the swap until saved again. Where: nornir-pyre/pyre/common.py (stos_image_dims_from_stos_config).

  • 2026-10-22 Pyre composite view: solve $T(\ell) = d$ instead of applying $T^{-1}$ once (formula above). Why: mesh forward and inverse triangulate different point sets and RBF fits two separate functions, so $T(T^{-1}(p))$ missed $p$ by up to 15 px (mesh) and 5 px (RBF) on a jittered 8x8 grid; each registration re-centred through it, and the view crept about 45 px over 40 results. With the solve the worst miss is 0.026 px (mesh, 300 points, mean about 6 single-point transforms) and 0.01 px (RBF); the re-centred framing is also pinned so it cannot move. Rejected: 4 steps (worst 0.88 px on mesh). Where: nornir-pyre/pyre/views/composite_display.py.

  • 2026-10-22 Pyre: a held Space registers each point once per hold. Why: key auto-repeat (about 30 presses/s) re-registered the hovered point every 100-200 ms, and point alignment is not idempotent at subpixel level: the point moved 0.3-1 px on every repeat indefinitely (process pool, synthetic image). The user chose once per point per hold over once per key press and over continuous re-registration. Where: nornir-pyre/pyre/controllers/control_point_registration_queue.py (SpaceHoldRegistrations).

  • 2026-10-26 Exporter window placement: quarter-step lattice rejected; free placement under consideration, not implemented. Why: on RPC2 (set v3, shapes.jsonl, outlines simplified so approximate) 2,388 of about 5,500 masks that fit one 1024 crop sit between half-step (512 px) lattice positions and are split by the tile-aligned phase search (location 29308: 641 x 419 px, split 6% / 94%). A quarter-step lattice would fix only 1,234 of them; centring a window on the mask (clamped to contain the padded bbox) fixes all. The trainer sees one image per example with no tile-grid awareness, and the client/server fuse aligned 1024 cells at inference, so whole-object crops match inference better than clipped fragments. Image sharing (group_windows, identical windows only) is 1.8% of RPC2 v3 images (953 of 52,332), so a mask-specific window costs little. Rejected: quarter-step lattice (user, 2026-10-26; half the cases remain). Open: effect on training is unmeasured; needs a v4 export and a fixed whole-mask validation set. Any change is a new set number (Training-set-naming.mdc). Where: nornir-buildmanager/nornir_buildmanager/operations/segmentationtraining/geometry.py (place_mask_windows).

  • 2026-10-26 Pyre point registration: send CPU transforms to pool workers; one hovered point per press. Why: on a real STOS pair (3248 points, CuPy) a point took 3.4-10.1 s because the GPU grid/mesh transform was pickled to each cold worker, which started CUDA and compiled kernels (2.6-2.9 s per worker); a CPU rebuild of the transform brings it to about 0.12 s warm and 0.5-0.6 s on a worker’s first task. Hover-and-Space also added the hovered point to the shared selection, so press N aligned N points; each press now aligns one. The CPU and GPU snapshots differ by a median of 0.7 px (max about 3 px on 30 points), within the run-to-run variation of about 0.9 px, and both are equally accurate against a synthetic ground truth. Rejected: killing in-flight pool workers on cancel (the pool is shared; results are discarded instead). Where: nornir-pyre/pyre/controllers/transformcontroller.py.

  • 2026-10-25 Carry at export, not in the trainer. One implementation of the carry for circles and polygons, matched to Viking’s production carry, with per-circle provenance so a new correction set or an edited annotation re-carries only what changed. All linked circles are exported with their overlap; the trainer filters (threshold changes need no re-export). Links alone decide what is carried: a link between a circle and a polygon means the process continues, so no terminal flag is needed.

  • 2026-10-25 Links must include circles. shapes.py links only polygon locations, so circle-polygon links were dropped. In the v5 data only 619 of 40,998 RPC1 locations and 581 of 13,024 RPC2 locations had a linked outline 1-2 sections away, and the trainer’s adjacent-polygon prompt realised 0.7% of its 15% share.

  • Exporter writes true circle radii (nornir 3b6fe2f removed -CircleDiameterScale): the scale is a training choice and can change without a re-export.

Discussion points

  • Measured pixel noise is shot-noise-shaped but far below what the camera’s counts-per-electron predicts. Binned variance against mean is linear (r 0.95-0.998). RC2 Gatan tiles (2012, nornir-testdata RC2_Micro/17): effective gain 1.2-2.1 counts/e⁻, read noise near 0, $\sigma^2/S$ 1.3-1.45. OneView tiles (Y:\AshCameraTest, Aug 2026; RPC3’s 2023 idoc records CountsPerElectron = 115.93 for that camera): effective gain 0.44-1.22, so pixel sigma is 130-155 counts where Poisson at 116 counts/e⁻ predicts ~1,600. Likely cause: the scintillator spreads each electron over many pixels (neighbour correlation 0.87-0.99, partly structure). So the step must come from measured pixel noise, not from CountsPerElectron; the latter is useful for expressing codes in electrons (one $k = 2.5$ OneView step is about 3 e⁻). (2026-10-30, photon_transfer.py in the session scratchpad.)

  • One section-wide step is coarse in dark regions and fine in bright ones because shot noise grows as $\sqrt{S}$; a variance-stabilising transform before quantizing would equalise it (open question below).

  • Registered (warped) images exported from Pyre’s Save Warped Image before nornir-pyre 4eb7418 (2026-10-26) are the target image warped by the source-to-target transform, not the source image in target space; they should be re-exported. When the swap was introduced is not established (verify).

  • Linked annotations across sections are sparse in the exports so far; link coverage, not model capacity, limits how much adjacent-section prompting can be trained.

Open questions for a paper

  • Carry accuracy: carried-centre error against Viking’s production carry and against the linked polygon, per volume.

  • ~~Mosaic grid refine vs ir-refine-grid: which per-pass step differs~~ answered 2026-10-31: the per-vertex estimator. Still to measure: whether the legacy estimator closes the gap on RC3 1061, 1300 and 1637 as on 0800.

  • Crushed tiles: effect on mosaic registration (seam MAE and offsets vs -NoCrush on the same sections), on SAM2 training and inference when both use crushed tiles, and visible banding in Leveled/Viking views (only 10-17 codes span the tissue). Whether an Anscombe-stabilised crush in electron units (gain from CountsPerElectron or a per-section photon-transfer fit) keeps fewer levels for the same fidelity.
